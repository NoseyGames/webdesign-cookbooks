# Nikola Radeski — make a personal portfolio feel personal

Source: [nikolaradeski.com](https://nikolaradeski.com/) · Reviewed: 2026-10-21 (America/Los_Angeles)

## Evidence and scope

**Visual-reviewed.** Inspected the desktop opening view and read the page's navigation and service descriptions. The background's implementation and the portrait's possible motion were not established. The local-time display and award language are observed presentation elements, not independently verified credentials.

## Observed

A portrait in a tall rounded rectangle divides the large first and last names. Supporting role text sits above the names, with location and time on the right. The pale background contains faceted geometric forms. A dark rounded navigation dock sits near the bottom of the viewport and ends with a contrasting orange contact action. The page text includes skills, an about section, portfolio, and reviews.

## Transferable lesson

**Interpretation:** a personal portfolio benefits from a clear human anchor. The portrait, name, role, and location communicate identity before the page expands into capabilities. The navigation dock gives a compact route through a long page without requiring a large conventional masthead.

## Implementation suggestion

Use an original three-part desktop composition with a portrait between two text groups. At narrow widths, restore a simple order: name, role, portrait, and action. If using a floating section-navigation dock, keep it bounded to the viewport and reserve content space beneath it. This is a proposed approach, not a claim about the site's code.

## Accessible adaptation and limits

Expose the full name as one meaningful heading even if it is visually split. Do not announce a constantly updating clock. Provide sensible image alternative text and ensure the dock never hides keyboard focus. This approach is less useful for a team organization where an individual portrait would misrepresent the brand or decision-maker.
