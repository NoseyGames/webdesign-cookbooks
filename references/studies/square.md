# Square — organize breadth around user decisions

Source: [Square US homepage](https://squareup.com/us/en) · Requested URL: [squareup.com](https://squareup.com/) · Reviewed: 2026-10-21 (America/Los_Angeles)

## Evidence and scope

**Visual-reviewed.** Read homepage content and inspected the desktop navigation and hardware section. The root URL redirected to the US English page. The initial hero was blank during the first capture, so no conclusions are drawn about its finished appearance. Mobile layouts, checkout, keyboard interaction, and performance were not tested.

## Observed

The navigation separates business types, products, hardware, and pricing, with account and sales actions on the opposite side. The hardware section gives devices large, isolated product images on pale panels, a section heading and short explanation, and explicit previous/next controls. The content continues through product capabilities, industry choices, AI, pricing, and a closing acquisition action. Page text includes a skip link and named carousel controls; their presence is not proof of complete accessibility.

## Transferable lesson

**Interpretation:** a broad product company can reduce cognitive load by offering more than one route through its information: what the customer does, what capability they need, and what device they want. Consistent product presentation makes hardware comparison easier even when the catalog is diverse.

## Implementation suggestion

Model navigation by audience and task before styling it. Give product cards consistent image ratios, names, short distinguishing descriptions, and links. Prefer a responsive grid; add horizontal browsing only where it improves a small-screen experience. This is an independent implementation proposal.

## Accessible adaptation and limits

Keep all products reachable without dragging, name carousel controls, expose current state, and preserve a logical reading order. Treat pricing and claims as maintained content, never decorative filler. This breadth of navigation would overwhelm a small product with one audience and one conversion goal.
